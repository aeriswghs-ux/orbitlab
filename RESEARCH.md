# Comparable tools and learning choices

Research date: 2026-11-23. This is a focused comparison of official descriptions and linked educational material, not an exhaustive market survey or usability study.

| Tool | Existing strengths | OrbitLab response |
|---|---|---|
| [PhET My Solar System](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/my-solar-system) and [official project](https://github.com/phetsims/my-solar-system) | Established interactive gravitational/orbital exploration | Focus on a small Earth-satellite learning sequence and transparent kilometer/second units rather than trying to replace its broad exploration |
| [Nebraska NAAP Planetary Orbits](https://astro.unl.edu/naap/pos/pos.html) | Kepler laws, orbital parameters, velocity/force relationships, student guides and instructor resources | Keep predict-observe-explain activity and conceptual feedback next to the model, with stable lesson URLs and direct preset links |

The following improvements are implemented relative to a bare animation, not claimed as novel inventions absent from every competitor:

1. **A measurable prediction:** baseline/current comparison and period ratio support a controlled fixed-a eccentricity experiment.
2. **Physical speed versus playback:** separate controls and explanations prevent the common misunderstanding that changing animation rate changes orbital energy.
3. **Unit and distance distinctions:** center distance, altitude, km/s, seconds/minutes and gravity conversion are explicit.
4. **Perspective literacy:** orbit-plane and equatorial projection modes explain foreshortening, a polar edge-on view, and false visual Earth crossings.
5. **Reproducible records:** CSV export includes numerical units and Earth constants.
6. **Readable learning content:** five independent HTML lessons, no account dependency, native keyboard controls, text summaries, and practice feedback that explains the answer.
7. **Inspectable assumptions:** visible simplified-model notes, sources, tests, and no claim of real-spacecraft observational validation.

## Next evidence to gather

Observe a small student group using the fixed-a prediction task; record misconceptions and completion difficulties with consent. Compare a short pre/post conceptual question set before claiming a learning benefit. Test with screen-reader users. Future scope could include an accessible true 3D view, variable argument of periapsis, and perturbation comparisons. Do not silently add those effects to the ideal model.
